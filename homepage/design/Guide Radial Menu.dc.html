01

Texture + red remap

The GLB's own base-colour map is kept. Pixels are walked, converted to HSL, and only the red hue band (h ≤ 26° or ≥ 332°, s ≥ 0.14) is rewritten — hue and saturation to the target, lightness re-centred on the target's own lightness. Everything outside the band passes through, so wear, panel lines and the belt survive.

You were right about the hue and wrong about where it hurts. Under the warm key at 4.0 flat cherry does not read near-black on cotton — measured off the rendered pixels it holds body Y 0.056 at 8.27:1, close to the token's 9.03:1 flat-field figure. It collapses on noir: 1.79:1, where he stops being a robot and becomes a silhouette on half the page. Chili holds noir at 2.81:1, but the hue reads pink-toy next to Archivo Black — compare 1B with the display line beside it.

Cherry hue and saturation, lightness re-centred on 0.25 with contrast × 1.18, then γ 0.68 across the curve: body Y 0.085 — 6.47:1 on cotton, 2.29:1 on noir. A 1.5× lift over flat cherry, which buys the noir half back without moving the hue; the shipped map's own Y is 0.135, so the robot sits one step under the original rather than five. Wear survives because contrast is boosted before the lift, not after. No new hex value enters the palette — the target is exactly --red-cherry, and only per-pixel lightness differs, which is what a texture is.

Parts stay at noir. With the remapped parts map the eye domes lift to a mid grey and the whole robot becomes red — the accent stops being an accent. Noir domes also give the mark something to sit in: the eye is the only bright thing on the model.

The eye goes to paper, and the measurement is blunt. In 1K the chili mark never crosses Y 0.45 at all — 4px² of the whole frame — because chili's luminance now sits below the body it has to mark. Pushing to 2.6 (1L) doubles that to 9px² by blooming, not by gaining edge. Paper at 1.35 measures 413px² at Y 0.98: one warm-white point in a dark dome on a red body, and the only version that still reads at 76px. Consequence, flagged: the eye is no longer a chili use, so the §6 accent budget changes (see the deviations list).

02

Robot framing in the puck

Framing is stated as the model-height span kept in the disc, not as a bare scalar, because this model is nearly all dome — the old worldH × 0.74 is the full-figure case. Second row is the squint test: the same render at 76px, blurred, judged as a silhouette.

2AFull figure — span −0.16→1.16 (= 0.74)
2BWaist-up — span 0.02→1.10
2CHead + shoulders — span 0.30→1.06
Locked — 2B waist-up, span 0.02→1.10 (robot height = 1.02 × disc height)

Full figure loses at 76px: the legs become two dark commas, the dome shrinks, and the squint test reads as a blob with no eye. Head-and-shoulders reads best as a silhouette but throws away the belt and the arms, so nothing in the disc changes when a clip plays — he stops looking alive.

Waist-up keeps the dome big enough that the paper mark is the first thing you read at 76px, keeps arm movement inside the frame, and puts the crown 16px below the disc's top edge at 104px — which is the headroom item 5 needs. Old constant retired: robotHeight = worldH × 0.74 becomes a framing span, so the same numbers hold at 104 and at 76 without re-tuning.

03

Puck treatment

Disc is noir on cotton and ink on noir, so the puck is always one step off its ground. Ring options below; the chili ring is not among them — with a red robot inside it is the fourth red in a 104px circle.

3ANo ring
3BHairline ring at rest
3CRing on hover / open only
Locked — ground-adaptive: 3A on cotton, 3B on noir, both step up on hover / open / drag

On cotton the noir disc already has 13:1 against the ground — a ring there is decoration, and decoration is what makes a puck look like a support widget. On noir the ink disc is only one step off the ground, so it needs a 1px gray #6E6963 edge to exist at all. Hover takes cotton on noir, hairline on cotton; drag takes gray on cotton; armed is the only red state.

Retired: the 1px chili ring at 55%. It was carrying the "there is red here" job that the body now does, and an opacity value is not in the token set anyway.

But six always-visible labels do not fit on the arc either — near the top the petals are 3px apart vertically, so per-petal captions collide. The answer is to split the two jobs: the ring carries the marks, and a right-aligned label index sits outside the fan at even 30px pitch, ordered so 01 pairs with the lowest petal. Next to Archivo Black and Newsreader, radius 2 is the only shape that survives. The cloud reads as a comic panel the moment it sits beside a 64px display line, and radius 10 puts the bubble in the same family as the Ask card, which then has to carry two different roles at two different radii. A squared slip with a hairline edge is the same object as the retired callout card — it just moved.

Tail: an 11px square rotated 45°, paper fill, hairline on the two exposed sides only, straddling the edge so the bubble's own border closes behind it. It slides along the bottom edge to sit under the crown, clamped 18px from either end; past that the bubble shifts and the tail pins at the clamp — no rubber-banding, no rotation. When the crown is within one bubble-height of the top edge the bubble flips under the puck and the tail moves to the top edge, still pointing at the head 5E. Content: eyebrow + one Newsreader line, no brief — 264px will not carry a paragraph without dropping under 15px, so the brief moves into the Ask panel where it has room. Position is written directly, never transitioned, so it cannot lag the head mid-drag; only opacity, clip-path and a 4px rise animate — 320ms --ease-out-sharp in, 200ms out.

06

Ask panel

One overlay at a time. Opening Ask retires the bubble and closes the ring; closing Ask brings the bubble back on the current chapter. Log copy below is placeholder — real answers come from site.ts.

Locked — 360px card, 300px log ceiling, question on cotton / answer in Newsreader

Bubble and panel cannot co-exist: they occupy the same 240px of air above his head and they say the same kind of thing. Ask suppresses the bubble in 160ms, then the panel clips up over 320ms. The ring closes at the same time — three layers open at once is the support-widget failure mode item 3 is trying to avoid.

Contrast between turns is ground, not colour: the question sits in a cotton block at Inter 14, the answer is bare paper in Newsreader 17 — the same split the page uses for eyebrow versus body, so nothing new is invented. Five exchanges scroll inside the log; the input row keeps its hairline top rule and stays pinned. At ≤520px the card becomes a sheet inset 12px with a 44vh log, and the puck drops to 76px in the corner. Chips are hairline outlines at Inter 11 uppercase — gray at rest, ink on hover. The retired callout brief lands here, as the first answer.

09

Deviations, flagged not folded in

  1. 01

    Accent budget §6 changes. The eye is no longer chili, and the body now carries cherry. Rendered red is: robot body field (cherry, film + live, counts once), nav-active underline, CTA email hover. The callout connector is gone. That is three, not four — and the chili graphic use on noir disappears from this component entirely.

  2. 02

    Petal size and arc changed without being asked. You specified 52px circles and a 90° arc; both had to move (44px, 100°) or six petals cannot fit at any of the three radii tested. Flagging rather than folding.

  3. 03

    Label index is a fourth option. You asked for three petal-content options; none of them fit six always-visible labels, so 4F pairs the ring with a separate label column. It is the recommendation, and it is new.

  4. 04

    Framing is a span, not a scalar. robotHeight = worldH × 0.74 is replaced by a kept-model-height span, because a single scalar cannot hold the same read at 104px and 76px on a model that is 70% dome.

  5. 05

    Bubble anchors to a measured crown offset. The Head_05 bone sits low inside the dome; projecting it alone puts the tail on his face. The offset from bone to model top is measured once at load and added in world space, so it still tracks the bone every frame.

  6. 06

    Ask log copy is placeholder. Five exchanges were needed to decide scroll behaviour; the answers are written from existing site copy but are not approved. Do not ship them.
