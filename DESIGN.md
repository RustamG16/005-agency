# CONVENIUM STUDIO — Stitch Design Brief

## Status

Approved visual reference: [Symbol Studio](https://www.symbolstudio.pl/en/).

This document defines the design and storytelling direction for the Stitch design phase. It is not an implementation specification and does not authorize the website build.

Brand: **Convenium Studio**. Use this name consistently in all generated layouts, headings, navigation, metadata and contact details.

## Core idea

**Take the business to another level.**

The elevator is not decoration. It is the organizing metaphor for the entire experience:

- The desert is the client's current design landscape: noisy, generic, exposed and directionless.
- The conflicting signs represent commoditized promises such as “generic” and “quality design.”
- The character is the client: aware that the current level is no longer enough.
- The elevator is Convenium Studio's strategic and creative process.
- The ascent is the transformation from visibility to consequence.
- Each following section is a new floor: point of view, work, capabilities and people.
- The footer is the invitation to go up.

The story is about elevation, not luxury transport. Avoid literal elevator icons, floor-button decorations and novelty UI. Express the metaphor through pacing, vertical motion, numbering, doors, thresholds and copy.

## Experience statement

The site should feel like **an original cinematic evolution of Symbol Studio's structural language**: comparable editorial precision, proximity and geometric control, rebuilt around Convenium's elevator narrative. Do not reproduce Symbol Studio's branding, copy, imagery or exact component designs.

The first viewport is deliberately cinematic and chromatic. After the elevator threshold, the interface becomes disciplined black, bone and muted gold. This visual compression demonstrates what the studio does: it turns noise into clarity.

## Narrative arc

### Chapter 0 — Ground level

**Media:** supplied desert elevator film.

**Purpose:** establish the problem before introducing the studio's answer.

**Primary copy:**

> Your brand is not stuck.  
> It is waiting for the right floor.

**Supporting copy:**

> Convenium Studio designs brands for companies that refuse to stay where they are.

The hero begins with minimal interface copy. The film carries the narrative. The CONVENIUM STUDIO wordmark may enter after the character commits to the elevator; it should not obscure the opening visual information.

### Chapter 1 — The doors close

The end of the hero film becomes a threshold. The screen briefly compresses toward black, as if the elevator doors have closed. This is a code-controlled transition, not a dissolve added to the video.

On the following black screen:

> GOING UP.

This is the first decisive studio statement.

### Chapter 2 — The ascent

The kinetic manifesto occupies three pinned full-screen states:

1. **LOUD.** — attention
2. **PRECISE.** — meaning
3. **UNFORGETTABLE.** — memory

The words arrive vertically with the weight of a lift stopping at a floor. Each locks firmly into place. No elastic bounce or playful overshoot.

Optional microcopy beside the sequence:

- `01 / ATTENTION`
- `02 / MEANING`
- `03 / MEMORY`

### Chapter 3 — Proof at every level

Selected work appears as a disciplined Symbol-inspired project grid. The section statement is:

> We do not decorate businesses.  
> We change the level at which they compete.

Four fictional cases:

| Project | Sector | Scope | Outcome line |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VANTA | Cultural platform | Brand system | From local signal to cultural frequency. |
| AUREL | Luxury hospitality | Identity and campaign | From a destination to a point of view. |
| NULL/ONE | Artificial intelligence | Strategy and digital | From technical capability to human relevance. |
| FERRO | Architecture | Naming and identity | From built space to lasting memory. |

At rest, cards show different crops of the gallery master frame. On hover, each reveals a different crop and time offset of the same continuous gallery film. This creates four distinct-feeling cards without generating four disconnected visual worlds.

### Chapter 4 — The machinery

Services are presented as a two-column editorial directory, subtly echoing floors without becoming themed UI.

| Level | Service | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | Strategy | Find the position worth owning. |
| 02 | Brand identity | Build the system that makes it visible. |
| 03 | Digital experiences | Make the system move and respond. |
| 04 | Campaigns | Put the idea into culture. |
| 05 | Motion | Give the brand a recognizable behavior. |

Left-column statement:

> Elevation is not one big move. It is a sequence of exact decisions.

### Chapter 5 — Inside the studio

The team film reveals the people behind the system. It should be human but not corporate, observed rather than posed.

**Heading:**

> SMALL TEAM.  
> SERIOUS LIFT.

**Body:**

> Strategists, designers and makers working close enough to keep the idea intact from first question to final frame.

### Chapter 6 — Next floor

The footer completes the elevator metaphor without a visual gag.

**Primary CTA:**

> GOT A BRAND WORTH FIGHTING FOR?  
> TAKE IT HIGHER.

Contact: `hello@convenium.studio`

Socials: Instagram, LinkedIn, Are.na.

## Desktop layout

- Design width: `1440px`
- Page-edge inset: `16px`
- Primary content inset: `40–48px`
- Main grid: 12 columns with `8px` gutters
- Hairlines: `1px`
- Navigation track: approximately `480 × 40px`
- Navigation controls: `8px` radius
- Project cards: approximately `448 × 580px`, `10px` radius
- Media inset radius: `8px`
- Reading columns: approximately `600px` maximum
- Full-screen story states: minimum `100svh`

Use thin editorial rules to group related content. Proximity should communicate hierarchy before borders or background cards do.

## Mobile layout

- Design width: `390px`
- Side gutter: `16–20px`
- Hero remains full-screen and video-led.
- Keep the character and elevator in the safe central crop.
- Replace the segmented navigation with `MENU`.
- Menu opens as a full-screen black takeover with large bone-white rows.
- Project cards become a single-column stack.
- Hover videos become tap-to-preview or short muted autoplay when cards enter the viewport.
- Manifesto words reduce in size but remain cropped and forceful.

## Navigation

### Desktop

- Original Convenium Studio wordmark or `CS` monogram at upper-left. If no approved monogram is supplied, use a clean text wordmark rather than inventing a complex logo.
- Fixed centered segmented control: `INDEX`, `WORK`, `SERVICES`, `STUDIO`, `CONTACT`.
- Black track; active segment is bone with black text.
- Upper-right contains a small circular `↑` indicator and a gold `START A PROJECT` control.
- The active segment slides rather than flashing between states.

### Mobile

- Monogram left.
- Compact gold project control and `MENU` right.
- Open state: black full-screen overlay, five large ruled navigation rows and a gold close control.

### Gold rule

Gold is used in exactly three interface treatments:

1. `START A PROJECT` control and its mobile equivalent.
2. Manifesto level indicator.
3. Final footer arrow or full stop.

The warm light and gold material already present inside the film are photographic content and are not counted as interface accents.

### Hero grade

The supplied hero is intentionally more colorful than the interface. Apply a restrained browser grade so the film still feels like the noisy “before” world:

- `brightness(0.78)`
- `saturate(0.78)`
- `contrast(1.08)`
- subtle black overlay, approximately `16–22%`

Do not turn the film monochrome. Its dusk color is part of the narrative contrast.

## Typography

Use licensed alternatives if available. For a build with open fonts:

| Role | Preferred direction | Open fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display | Wide brutalist grotesk | Archivo Black |
| Editorial serif | Refined, high-contrast but readable | Newsreader |
| UI and metadata | Neutral compact grotesk | Inter |

Rules:

- Oversized display words can bleed beyond the viewport.
- Display line-height: `0.84–0.92`.
- Body serif line-height: approximately `1.25–1.35`.
- Micro-labels are uppercase, `11–13px`, with restrained tracking.
- Do not use the proprietary Rules font from the reference site without a license.

## Media treatment

- Hero video: full-bleed, scroll-scrubbed, never inside a card.
- Gallery film: one continuous `16:9` master reused through four card crops.
- Team film: full-width dark panel with copy in a protected negative-space area.
- All website video is silent, muted, inline and free of embedded captions.
- Avoid AI-generated readable words in new media. Add typography in the interface.
- Media corners follow the `8–10px` radius system outside the hero.

## Motion system

| Interaction | Timing | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nav hover | `180ms` | Fast color and position response |
| Active nav slider | `260ms` | Sharp ease-out |
| Project hover reveal | `320ms` | A vertical mask reveals video beneath the poster; media scales to `1.035` |
| Manifesto word arrival | `420ms` | Vertical impact and hard stop |
| Mobile menu | `450ms` | Overlay and ruled rows enter in sequence |
| Cursor trail | `120–180ms` follow | Small gold dot with restrained ring lag |

The hero follows the supplied film frame-for-frame through scroll. Do not add cuts, crossfades or blurred dissolves. The transition after the final frame is built in code and holds a sharp frame boundary.

## Accessibility and performance

- Maintain WCAG AA text contrast.
- Preserve native focus states, restyled to match the system.
- Keyboard users must reach all navigation, cards and contact links.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` replaces scrub and slams with poster frames and opacity changes.
- Provide a static hero poster for low-power devices and reduced motion.
- Use responsive media sources and lazy-load non-hero video.
- The four work cards reuse one video asset instead of downloading four copies.
- Mobile replaces hover-only meaning with visible labels and tap/viewport behavior.

### Stitch static-media rule

For the Stitch design phase, **do not place or generate playable video**. The user will upload screenshots extracted from all three approved videos. Use those uploaded screenshots as the exact static visual stand-ins for their corresponding animated sections:

1. The desert/elevator screenshot represents the full-viewport hero video.
2. The columns/gallery screenshot represents the work-card hover video.
3. The creative-team screenshot represents the full-width team video.

Do not replace, redraw, recolor, reinterpret or regenerate these uploaded screenshots. Preserve their composition and photographic identity. Cropping is allowed only where this document explicitly calls for responsive or project-card crops.

Show motion through separate static state frames and written interaction annotations, not through invented visual effects. The real MP4 files will replace these screenshots during implementation.

If more than one screenshot from the same video is uploaded, treat them as sequential states of one continuous shot—not as separate scenes or a collage.

## Stitch image-generation direction

Generate original supporting stills for the design. All stills must feel as if they belong to the same Convenium Studio campaign.

### Shared photographic language

Photorealistic European creative-studio art direction. Controlled, editorial and tactile. Deep black, warm bone white, dark natural wood, brushed metal and extremely restrained old-gold details. Precise compositions, strong negative space, subtle 35mm film grain, realistic materials, rich blacks with preserved detail and soft highlight rolloff. No orange, cyan or magenta accents outside the supplied hero film. No watermarks, captions, fake logos, legible AI-generated words, malformed typography, generic stock-photo smiles or glossy science-fiction styling.

Typography, project names and labels must be added by the interface, not baked into generated images.

### Work covers

Generate four distinct `4:5` project covers. These are the default states of the work cards. In the static Stitch hover-state frame, a vertical mask reveals a crop of the uploaded gallery/columns screenshot beneath them. During implementation, that screenshot is replaced by the supplied gallery video.

1. **VANTA — Cultural platform:** a nighttime cultural installation inside a severe concrete city passage; oversized black-and-bone poster planes, abstract unreadable typographic forms, distant silhouettes and one restrained old-gold light.
2. **AUREL — Hospitality:** a quiet hospitality identity still life with bone stationery, black room key, textured paper, dark glass vessel and one brushed-gold architectural object on natural stone.
3. **NULL/ONE — Artificial intelligence:** a credible physical data installation in a black gallery with translucent glass panels, a precise grid of white lights, one sculptural black monolith and subtle gold reflections; sophisticated, not science-fiction.
4. **FERRO — Architecture:** an architect's project table with a concrete building model, blackened steel samples, tracing paper, a bone identity book and drawing instruments under strong directional light.

### Services and studio mosaic

Create an editorial mosaic inspired by the density and image rhythm of a design-studio journal, without copying the reference site. Use a combination of portraits, process and architecture:

1. `3:4` dark editorial portrait of a creative director in three-quarter profile inside the studio.
2. `3:4` window-side portrait of a strategist reviewing one printed sheet.
3. `4:5` two-person layout review at a large working table, observed rather than posed.
4. `4:5` pin-up wall with abstract identity studies, material swatches and grids; one person seen from behind adjusts a print.
5. `16:9` empty studio interior after hours with a long shared table, print shelves, city bokeh and a calm dark region for copy.
6. `3:2` close detail of hands aligning a bone layout against a metal ruler, surrounded by black paper and tactile samples.

Human imagery must feel like one small, consistent team in one consistent studio. Faces should be natural and secondary to the work. Avoid direct eye contact except in one intentional portrait.

### Contact imagery

Generate two invitation images:

1. `16:9` dark architectural doorway standing open inside a minimal studio corridor, with warm bone light beyond and restrained gold hardware. It suggests the next level without showing a literal elevator.
2. `4:5` meeting table prepared for one new client: two dark chairs, one closed bone presentation folio, a black pencil and water, with directional window light and quiet anticipation.

### Image placement

- Work covers: one image per project card.
- Service imagery: place as a low horizontal strip or editorial mosaic beneath the two-column service copy.
- Studio imagery: combine portraits, process details and interior frames into an asymmetric two-column mosaic beside the studio statement.
- Contact imagery: use the architectural threshold as the primary panel and the meeting table as the smaller supporting crop.
- Preserve `8–10px` media radii and `8px` gaps.
- Do not add imagery to the kinetic manifesto or oversized footer. Those sections depend on typographic force and negative space.

## Stitch workflow

**Never paste this document into Stitch.** Stitch drops components beyond ~5,000 characters, cannot parse tables, and "do not" instructions backfire. This file is the master reference for humans and for the code build.

For Stitch, use `STITCH-PROMPTS.md`: a sequenced set of short copy-paste prompts (one section or one change per prompt, reference screenshots attached at named steps). Deliverables, order and recovery steps are defined there.

## Non-negotiables

- The elevator story must remain understandable without explanatory copy.
- The post-hero interface must become visibly more controlled than the desert world.
- Do not copy Symbol Studio's logo, Rules font, project content or orange identity.
- No generic SaaS cards, gradients, glass blobs or decorative 3D icons.
- No cuts or dissolves inside scroll-scrub media.
- Do not begin implementation until the Stitch design is approved.
