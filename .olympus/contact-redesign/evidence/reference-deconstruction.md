# Reference deconstruction — Symbol Studio contact

## Source

- Live reference: https://www.symbolstudio.pl/en/contact
- User-supplied screenshots: `codex-clipboard-40c21f9f-74ae-482c-adcd-e6fdbccf4d77.png` and `codex-clipboard-55da8636-3cc3-43a8-8968-1d09be94ccbe.png`
- Captured evidence: `reference-symbolstudio-desktop.png` and `reference-symbolstudio-mobile-settled.png`

## Visual thesis

An availability-led, whitespace-heavy contact experience centers a direct invitation, then pairs an accountable human contact path with a quiet, linear form.

## System observations

- **Composition:** A large white content field sits inside a cool-gray shell. Desktop uses a centered hero followed by a two-column contact/form area; mobile becomes a direct vertical sequence.
- **Typography:** Large uppercase sans-serif type carries the hero; small uppercase labels and neutral body copy maintain a restrained, operational tone.
- **Color/material:** Black, white, cool gray, and a bright orange action accent create crisp separation with minimal surface decoration.
- **Media:** A contact-person portrait is the main trust asset. It is functional rather than atmospheric.
- **Interaction grammar:** Navigation and primary CTAs use compact segmented controls; form fields use lightweight underline treatments and a large submit control.
- **Content behavior:** Availability, invitation, reassurance, named contact, form, address, and global capability proof appear in that order.
- **Responsive behavior:** The live mobile page reduces hero type substantially, swaps to a shorter hero phrase, and stacks contact details before the form.
- **Risks:** Very large empty fields can make the form appear below the fold; low-contrast gray text and animation entry states need care; exact headline, shell, segmented navigation, and orange controls are distinctive expressions that must not be copied.

## Contradictions and transformation needs

- Symbol Studio’s cool gray, black, turquoise, and orange palette conflicts with Convenium’s locked cotton, noir, cherry, maroon, and chili system.
- The reference sells branding/rebranding guidance; Convenium needs to sell premium web-product strategy to hospitality, real-estate, and adjacent luxury decision-makers.
- The reference can lean on a named sales representative and portrait. Convenium should not imitate that trust pattern unless equivalent verified assets are supplied.
- The reference uses a simple low-qualification form. Convenium needs enough qualification to protect sales time, but only after establishing consultation value.

## Asset and motion implications

- Contact-person media is optional, not assumed. If the selected direction requires it, the user should supply a rights-cleared portrait and verified contact details.
- Client proof or a relevant premium-sector case study could replace portrait-based trust if approved assets exist.
- A restrained status/hero reveal is a motion candidate. No WebGL, scroll narrative, or new animation dependency is justified by this reference.

## Keep / transform / avoid

- **Keep:** Immediate availability, single dominant invitation, reassurance before form, accountable fallback, quiet inputs.
- **Transform:** Brand palette, typography, voice, form qualification, proof strategy, and mobile headline behavior.
- **Avoid:** Copying the headline, centered white-panel shell, segmented navigation, orange CTA language, named personnel, or exact form composition.
