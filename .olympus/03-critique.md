## Evidence and fatal risks

### Concept 1 — The Material Monolith

- **Strongest evidence:** It directly repairs the audit’s highest-priority defect—the empty centerpiece—while preserving the existing WebGL architecture, typographic system, grid, and scroll behavior the user already likes. It aligns technical proof with the audience seeking premium interactive products.
- **Weakest assumption:** Luxury buyers will read material transformation as relevant business evidence rather than decorative 3D. That depends on disciplined copy, real proof plates, and clear capability mapping.
- **Fatal risk, if any:** If the cover, plates, and material maps are low-contrast, visually inconsistent, or over-compressed, the result will repeat the current dark-empty problem with more technical cost. Excess shader/texture ambition could also make mobile fallback feel like a lesser experience.
- **Weighted rationale:** Strongest brand continuity and art-direction fit; clear desktop hierarchy and a credible mobile adaptation; high originality without changing the stack. It loses usability points because a long scrub narrative still delays proof and CTA.
- **Confidence and missing evidence:** High confidence in stack fit; medium confidence in final visual quality until representative assets and a mobile GPU test exist. No conversion baseline or final portraits are available.

### Concept 2 — Two Hands, One Studio

- **Strongest evidence:** It answers the audit’s founder-trust problem most directly and offers the clearest, shortest explanation of who owns each discipline. Its regular HTML/media structure gives it the strongest accessibility, mobile, and performance outlook.
- **Weakest assumption:** Personal founder access is a stronger luxury buying trigger than visible technical spectacle. No user research or inquiry data confirms which differentiator carries more weight.
- **Fatal risk, if any:** Without exceptional, coherent portrait and process photography, the design becomes a conventional two-founder agency page and undersells the real-time 3D capability central to the desired market position.
- **Weighted rationale:** Best usability and lowest production risk, with excellent content clarity. It scores below Concept 1 on brand-specific creativity and on demonstrating the high-end WebGL products the target audience is expected to value.
- **Confidence and missing evidence:** High confidence in implementation feasibility; medium confidence in premium differentiation until final photography is reviewed.

### Concept 3 — The Private Viewing

- **Strongest evidence:** It is the most immediately specific to hotels and real estate and has the clearest luxury-service metaphor. The mobile plan correctly requires a separate vertical composition rather than cropping desktop media.
- **Weakest assumption:** A fictional generated property will feel like authored brand storytelling rather than generic luxury advertising or implied client work.
- **Fatal risk, if any:** Temporal inconsistency, artificial architecture, embedded-screen artifacts, or poor video scrubbing could undermine credibility. Producing both desktop and mobile films before visual continuity is locked creates substantial schedule and rework risk.
- **Weighted rationale:** Highest novelty and strong art direction, but the most asset-dependent experience. Comprehension and performance rely on carefully timed camera holds and device-specific encoding; content proof is weaker because generated environments cannot substitute for real outcomes.
- **Confidence and missing evidence:** Medium confidence. No film prototype, generation tests, compression budget, or target-device decoding measurements exist.

## Recommendation

**Select Concept 1 — The Material Monolith.** It offers the best fit to the approved goal: attracting luxury businesses that value high-end scroll animation and real-time 3D while keeping the current page’s strongest design DNA. It fixes the empty-media problem through a bounded extension of existing technology rather than making the entire experience dependent on a generated film.

Concept 2 is the safest alternative if authentic founder photography becomes the dominant available asset or schedule is tight. Concept 3 should be selected only if the user explicitly accepts the largest media-production and continuity risk.

This critique does not merge or revise the frozen concepts. Gate B remains pending.
