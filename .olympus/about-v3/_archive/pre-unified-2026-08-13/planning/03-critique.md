## Evidence and fatal risks

### Concept 1 — The Private Briefing

- **Strongest evidence-backed advantage:** Directly answers the premium buyer's emotional risk—who listens, interprets and remains accountable—matching the audit's unique About opportunity without reproducing Services, Work or Contact.
- **Weakest assumption:** Excellent two-founder photography, process footage and a disciplined showreel will exist; the audit identifies these as structurally missing.
- **Fatal risk, if any:** Weak or unavailable human media makes the spacious editorial composition feel empty and leaves trust resting on claims.
- **Weighted score rationale:** Strongest narrative hierarchy and CTA coherence; responsive substitutions are explicit. Creativity is lower because the premium editorial “private meeting” model is familiar, although it is well translated into Convenium's identity.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.
- **Missing evidence:** Verified Marija biography/languages, complete portrait package, showreel inputs and rights, real form backend, and validation of the limited-capacity claim.

### Concept 2 — Two Lenses, One Direction

- **Strongest evidence-backed advantage:** The two-founder relationship becomes a distinctive, brand-specific information and interaction system; multilingual interpretation is made concrete rather than decorative.
- **Weakest assumption:** Equivalent credible evidence exists for both founders. The audit currently supports Rustam much more strongly and explicitly flags Marija's biography/media as unverified.
- **Fatal risk, if any:** Unequal founder proof collapses the central symmetry into an artificial device, making Marija appear like narrative balancing rather than substantiated authority.
- **Weighted score rationale:** Highest design-system coherence and originality. Usability loses points because split reading, seam choreography, R/M/WE mobile tabs and a three-column translation table increase cognitive and responsive complexity. The proposed buyer statements are unsupported by the current absence of client research and would need to be explicitly fictional examples rather than “real” evidence.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Missing evidence:** Verified Marija artifacts and role history, confirmed language ownership, paired media, rights-cleared diptych footage, a mobile interaction prototype and validated example language.

### Concept 3 — The Studio Journal

- **Strongest evidence-backed advantage:** Most rigorous proof/provenance posture; clearly labelled career, independent and experimental artifacts could establish credibility without inventing Convenium client history.
- **Weakest assumption:** Enough consistently strong, rights-cleared artifacts exist to sustain an evidence cabinet and long editorial page.
- **Fatal risk, if any:** Sparse or weak evidence turns the journal into filler, while the fictional hotel brief becomes the page's dominant “proof” and undermines the trust goal.
- **Weighted score rationale:** Strong art direction and originality, but the densest scan burden and latest conversion path. The issue index, filters, lateral artifact movement, expandable standards and simulated brief create more interaction and content-production risk than the current evidence supports.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Missing evidence:** Full artifact inventory and permissions, Marija materials, showreel master, provenance policy, evidence that the audience wants journal-level depth, and mobile readability testing.

## Recommendation

**Recommend Concept 1 — The Private Briefing.** It best addresses the approved trust and qualified-inquiry goal with the least dependence on unavailable proof. Concept 2 ties on raw quality but carries substantially more evidence dependency and responsive complexity. Concept 3 is not justified by the current asset base.

No concepts were merged or revised during critique.
